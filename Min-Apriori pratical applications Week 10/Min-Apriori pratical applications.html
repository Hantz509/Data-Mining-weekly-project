Min-Apriori Algorithm for Quantitative Data

The standard Apriori algorithm was developed primarily for binary transactional databases, focusing on the discrete presence or absence of items. However, in disciplines such as Text Mining and Document Analysis, datasets frequently comprise quantitative or continuous attributes, such as the normalized frequency of terms within a document.

When analyzing a document-term matrix, applying traditional binarization results in a significant loss of frequency information. Standard discretization is similarly suboptimal, as it clusters similar frequencies rather than measuring true topological co-occurrence. The Min-Apriori algorithm elegantly resolves this limitation by evaluating the intensity of variable co-occurrence utilizing a bounding minimum threshold, thereby ensuring statistical significance is prioritized over mere existence.

Examine the Methodology ↓

The Minimum Co-occurrence Principle

Min-Apriori evaluates joint presence by constraining a pair of attributes to the strength of its least prominent member. For instance, if one attribute demonstrates high significance (e.g., a weight of $0.80$) while another is marginal (e.g., $0.20$), the $\min()$ function accurately reflects their joint significance as bounded by the lesser value ($0.20$).

Formal Definition of Support

Given any two quantitative items $A$ and $B$ in document $d$ with continuous weights $w_{d,A}$ and $w_{d,B}$, the joint support within that document is defined as:

$$Sup_d(\{A, B\}) = \min(w_{d,A}, w_{d,B})$$

Algorithmic Methodology

Implementation Steps

1

Data Preparation (Conditional)

For continuous attributes (e.g., word frequencies), normalize the quantitative vectors (utilizing $L_1$ norms) to ensure the total support of each attribute equals $1.0$. For discrete integer transactions (e.g., retail purchase quantities), this step is bypassed and raw values are used directly.

2

Parameter Initialization

Establish a Minimum Support (min_sup) threshold. This parameter acts as a pruning filter to determine whether an itemset possesses statistical significance, eliminating weak associations.

3

Support for 1-Itemsets ($L_1$)

Identify frequent single attributes by aggregating their quantitative values across the dataset. Retain only the variables where the total sum meets or exceeds the defined min_sup constraint.

4

Candidate 2-Itemsets ($C_2$)

Construct sets of candidate pairs $\{W_1, W_2\}$. To compute joint support, identify the minimum quantitative value between the two attributes per document/transaction, sum these global minimums, and prune pairs falling below min_sup.

5

Candidate K-Itemsets ($C_k$)

Iteratively combine frequent $(k-1)$-itemsets to synthesize $k$-itemsets. Extract the minimum value among all $k$ items per transaction, calculate the aggregate sum, and prune against min_sup until the candidate set is empty.

6

Rule Extraction

Derive directional association rules (e.g., $A \rightarrow B$) from the frequent itemsets, assessing their predictive validity via the Confidence metric.

Case Study 1

Application 1: Retail Market Basket Analysis (Purchase Quantities)

In retail environments, standard Apriori only detects whether an item was purchased (binary state). Min-Apriori extends this framework to analyze the intensity of associations based on varying purchase quantities. Consider a supermarket analyzing five customer transactions ($T_1 \dots T_5$) involving four products ($A, B, C, D$). We establish a significance threshold of min_sup = 3.

Transaction ID Item A Item B Item C Item D
$T_1$ 3 2 5 -
$T_2$ 1 4 - 2
$T_3$ - 1 3 1
$T_4$ 2 - 2 3
$T_5$ - 2 1 -

1. Base Item Support ($L_1$)

Aggregate the independent support values. For example, Item B appears in $T_1, T_2, T_3,$ and $T_5$:

$$Sup(\{B\}) = 2 + 4 + 1 + 2 = \mathbf{9}$$

Evaluating against min_sup = 3, all individual items $\{A, B, C, D\}$ exhibit frequent prevalence and proceed to candidate generation.

2. Candidate Itemsets ($C_2 \rightarrow C_3$)

We calculate joint co-occurrence utilizing the minimum bounding function. For the pair $\{B, C\}$ appearing jointly in $T_1, T_3,$ and $T_5$:

$$Sup(\{B, C\}) = \min(2,5) + \min(1,3) + \min(2,1) = 2 + 1 + 1 = \mathbf{4}$$

While pairs like $\{B, C\}$ satisfy the threshold ($4 \ge 3$), higher-order sets face rigorous pruning. For example, $\{A, B, C\}$ yields a support of only $2$ (derived exclusively from $T_1$), thereby failing the minimum threshold.

3. Confidence Assessment

Deriving the predictive probability for the rule $B \rightarrow C$:

$$Conf(B \rightarrow C) = \frac{Sup(\{B, C\})}{Sup(\{B\})} = \frac{4}{9} \approx \mathbf{44.4\%}$$

Conclusion: Within this quantitative dataset, if a customer purchases item B, there is an empirically validated 44.4% confidence that they will also purchase item C in statistically significant, corresponding quantities.

Case Study 2

Application 2: Document-Term Matrix Evaluation

Consider an application of Min-Apriori to a simplified Document-Term Matrix comprising three documents ($D_1, D_2, D_3$) and two target terms ($W_A, W_B$). Assuming the preliminary step is complete, the word vectors are L1-normalized across the corpus such that the total support for each word equals $1.0$. We define the significance threshold at min_sup = 0.4.

Document Term A ($W_A$) Term B ($W_B$) Min($W_A, W_B$)
Document 1 0.5 0.2 0.2
Document 2 0.3 0.6 0.3
Document 3 0.2 0.2 0.2
Total Support ($\sum$) 1.0 1.0 0.7

1. Joint Support Computation

To compute the support of the 2-itemset $\{W_A, W_B\}$, the algorithm aggregates the minimum frequency distributions across the document set:

$$Sup(W_A, W_B) = \sum_{d \in D} \min(w_{d,A}, w_{d,B})$$
$$Sup(W_A, W_B) = 0.2 + 0.3 + 0.2 = \mathbf{0.7}$$

✓ As $0.7 \ge 0.4$ (min_sup), $\{W_A, W_B\}$ is classified as a frequent itemset.

2. Rule Confidence Derivation

Next, we extract the association rule $W_A \rightarrow W_B$. The probability of this inference is calculated by dividing the joint support by the antecedent's individual support:

$$Conf(A \rightarrow B) = \frac{Sup(A, B)}{Sup(A)}$$
$$Conf(W_A \rightarrow W_B) = \frac{0.7}{1.0} = \mathbf{0.70}$$

This empirical metric indicates a 70% confidence level that the thematic presence of Term A implies the statistically significant co-occurrence of Term B within the same document context.

Methodological Interpretation & Significance

The derived confidence metric underscores the primary analytical advantage of the Min-Apriori framework. Had standard binarization been applied (e.g., mapping any frequency $> 0$ to $1$), the algorithm would erroneously equate a marginal footnote with a dominant central theme, leading to an artificially inflated confidence score.

By utilizing the minimum bounding function, Min-Apriori successfully verifies that Term A and Term B not only co-occur, but exhibit a statistically significant joint intensity. Consequently, the extracted association rules reflect robust thematic correlations, rendering this approach highly effective for text mining, topic modeling, and the analysis of highly skewed quantitative distributions where the magnitude of occurrence is paramount.
